\begin{abstract}
News-driven stock prediction is difficult because financial articles affect prices through structured events, delayed reactions, and market-wide interactions rather than through sentiment alone. Existing neural approaches often encode each article as a dense text vector or sentiment score, which weakens both temporal reasoning and interpretability. We present \textsc{CausalStock}, a three-stage framework that represents financial news as structured events, learns a sparse lag-aware event-type dependency graph, and uses the resulting event structure for stock movement prediction. We deliberately begin from data: an exploratory analysis of a 5\% subset of FNSPID reveals heavy headline duplication ($\approx$29\%), large publisher and author missingness (79\% and 68\%) against near-complete URLs, and predominantly sector- or market-wide article scope ($\approx$89\%). These three findings directly motivate three event attributes beyond the standard $(\text{subject},\text{action},\text{object},\text{magnitude})$ quadruple: \emph{novelty}, \emph{credibility}, and \emph{scope}. The extraction stage produces 10{,}901 structured events over a 20-class ontology. A downstream study over 9{,}566 ticker-aligned examples from 22 stocks shows that a tabular price-event-ticker model reaches 0.693 macro-F1 under stratified evaluation, and that a causal ensemble fusing price and event features with the learned event-type causal graph improves this to 0.709 -- a modest but consistent gain. Under chronological evaluation, all models fall to roughly 0.34 macro-F1, near the three-class random baseline, exposing temporal distribution shift as the central open challenge. We position the work as complementary to the NeurIPS 2024 CausalStock, which models stock-level causality, and give an interpretable comparison along data, method, and protocol axes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

News-driven stock prediction asks whether market movements can be forecast from recent financial news and historical price behavior. The task is useful for event monitoring, risk analysis, and decision support, but it is technically fragile: a news item rarely acts as a standalone signal. An interest-rate announcement, an earnings surprise, or an analyst downgrade can affect a stock immediately, after several trading days, or indirectly through sector and supply-chain relations.

Most existing financial text models simplify this setting by compressing each article into a sentiment label, a contextual embedding, or an attention-pooled representation \cite{araci2019,xu2018stocknet,hu2018han,sawhney2020man,sawhney2021fast,xu2021rest}. These representations are useful, but they blur three pieces of information that are central to event-driven markets: \emph{what} event occurred, \emph{which} entity it affected, and \emph{when} its downstream effect should arrive. Event-based methods recover some of this structure \cite{ding2014structured,ding2015deep,zhou2021trade}, yet they typically do not learn a global lag-aware dependency graph over event types.

This paper studies the following question: can financial news be modeled as a structured event process whose event types have learnable delayed dependencies, and can that structure improve the analysis of stock movement prediction? We use the term \emph{causal} in a predictive temporal sense: an edge from one event type to another indicates directed, time-preceded predictive influence under the observed data and regularization assumptions. We do not claim intervention-level identifiability from observational market data.

A guiding principle of this project is that schema design should be driven by data rather than assumed in advance. We therefore start with an exploratory data analysis (EDA) of the raw news corpus and let its quality characteristics dictate which event attributes are worth extracting. This analysis yields one foundational observation -- the corpus is event-dense, so an event representation is justified over a sentiment score -- and three concrete schema decisions, described in Section~\ref{sec:eda}.

We propose \textsc{CausalStock}, a three-stage framework for event-structured stock prediction. First, raw financial articles are converted into structured financial events whose attributes are chosen from the EDA findings. Second, a differentiable lag-aware causal discovery module learns an event-type strength matrix and lag matrix using sparse temporal attention with acyclicity regularization. Third, a tabular ensemble fuses the learned causal-graph features with event statistics and price history for movement prediction.

\textbf{Distinction from concurrent work.} A NeurIPS 2024 paper of the same name, CausalStock \cite{li2024causalstock}, learns causal graphs between individual \emph{stocks} via variational inference and a functional causal model. In contrast, our system learns causal graphs between 20 \emph{event types} and then propagates event-level shocks onto stocks. The two formulations are complementary: event-type causality is transferable across companies and markets, whereas stock-level causality is bound to a specific company universe. We treat \cite{li2024causalstock} as a reference point and analyze the performance gap in Section~\ref{sec:comparison}.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) An EDA-driven event schema in which three attributes -- novelty, credibility, scope -- are each justified by a measured property of the corpus. (ii) A reproducible pipeline spanning LLM silver labelling with rule- and market-grounded corrections, lag-aware event-type causal discovery, and a causal-feature prediction layer. (iii) An empirical analysis showing that the causal ensemble improves on a strong tabular baseline (0.709 vs.\ 0.693 macro-F1), and an honest separation of stratified-split information content from forward-time predictability, which collapses to near-random under chronological evaluation.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Financial text and event-driven prediction.} Prior financial NLP work shows that domain-specific language representations and event-based representations can both support stock movement analysis \cite{araci2019,xu2018stocknet,hu2018han,sawhney2020man,sawhney2021fast,xu2021rest,koa2024sep,ding2014structured,ding2015deep,chen2019finegrained,zhou2021trade,chen2024efsa}. Classical finance studies further establish that the informational content of news exceeds raw tone \cite{tetlock2007media,heston2015news} and that abnormal returns concentrate around discrete corporate events \cite{mackinlay1997event,alvarado2021stock,wu2025press}. \textsc{CausalStock} follows the event-based line but treats event types as nodes in a learned temporal dependency graph instead of reducing each article to sentiment alone.

\textbf{Temporal causal discovery.} Prior causal-discovery work motivates our use of time precedence, sparsity, and acyclicity when learning directed temporal relations \cite{granger1969,zheng2018notears,tank2022,nauta2019}. At the same time, causal discovery can exploit artificial benchmark structure \cite{reisach2021beware}; we therefore frame our graph as a predictive temporal structure rather than a fully identified causal model.

\textbf{Continuous-time models and weak supervision.} Neural ODEs and related continuous-time models provide tools for irregularly sampled propagation \cite{chen2018node,rubanova2019latent,kidger2020neural}, and point-process models connect event streams with self-attention \cite{mei2017,zuo2020}. For supervision, LLM-generated annotations have been shown to rival crowd labels on several NLP tasks \cite{wang2021gpt3}, and finance-specific LLMs extend this to financial text \cite{yang2023fingpt}. Our novelty and credibility attributes build on news-impact studies \cite{sano2017novel} and source-factuality estimation \cite{baly2018predicting}.

\section{Problem Definition}

Let $\mathcal{N}=\{n_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be a financial news stream. Each article is represented as $n_i=(x_i,t_i,\tau_i)$, where $x_i$ is the article text, $t_i$ is the publication time, and $\tau_i$ is the set of affected tickers. The event extraction problem is to learn a mapping
\begin{equation}
\phi(x_i,t_i,\tau_i)=e_i=(k_i,q_i,\boldsymbol{\pi}_i,\tau_i,t_i),
\end{equation}
where $k_i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$ is an event type, $q_i=(S_i,A_i,O_i,M_i)$ is a subject-action-object-magnitude quadruple, and $\boldsymbol{\pi}_i=(\text{polarity},\text{surprise},\text{scope},\text{novelty},\text{credibility})$ stores auxiliary impact attributes. In our experiments, $K=20$.

The temporal causal discovery problem is to learn two global matrices over event types:
\begin{equation}
A\in(0,1)^{K\times K}, \qquad T_{\mathrm{lag}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{K\times K}.
\end{equation}
The entry $A_{ab}$ measures the directed predictive strength from event type $a$ to event type $b$, while $T_{\mathrm{lag},ab}$ is the expected delay of that influence. Because the graph is learned from observational market data, these parameters are structured predictive dependencies under the model, not interventionally identified effects.

The stock prediction problem is defined over ticker-aligned examples. For stock $s$ and prediction day $d$, let $\mathcal{E}_{s,d}$ be the previous event context and let $P_{s,d-W_p:d}\in\mathbb{R}^{W_p\times 5}$ be the OHLCV price window. The next-day return is $r_{s,d+1}=(C_{s,d+1}-C_{s,d})/C_{s,d}$, where $C_{s,d}$ is the close price. We use a three-class label with threshold $\delta=0.005$:
\begin{equation}
y_{s,d+1}=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{UP}, & r_{s,d+1}>\delta,\\
\mathrm{DOWN}, & r_{s,d+1}<-\delta,\\
\mathrm{FLAT}, & \mathrm{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The goal is to predict $y_{s,d+1}$ from $(\mathcal{E}_{s,d},P_{s,d-W_p:d},s)$ while preserving interpretable event-level structure.

The analysis yields one foundational observation and three schema decisions.

\textbf{(F0) The corpus is event-dense, which justifies an event representation over sentiment.} Table~\ref{tab:eda_clues} shows that 88.0\% of articles contain explicit price-movement language, 77.4\% contain an expectation or numeric comparison, and 56.6\% concern earnings or revenue, with further sizable shares for M\&A, dividends and buybacks, analyst ratings, and legal or regulatory matters. The subset is therefore not a stream of diffuse opinion but a stream of discrete, typable corporate and macro events that carry quantitative arguments. Compressing each article into a single sentiment scalar would discard three recoverable and decision-relevant facts: the event \emph{type}, its numeric \emph{magnitude}, and the \emph{affected entity}. This measured composition is the empirical justification for the central design decision of this work -- extracting structured $(\text{type},\text{subject},\text{object},\text{magnitude})$ events under the 20-class ontology of Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} rather than scoring sentiment -- and the schema is matched to the corpus, not assumed in advance. The remaining three findings refine \emph{which} auxiliary attributes are worth extracting.

\textbf{(F1) Heavy headline duplication motivates \emph{novelty}.} About 29\% of stock-candidate rows repeat an earlier headline. The first report of an event carries the largest informational shock, while later restatements and syndication mostly amplify reach with diminishing marginal effect \cite{sano2017novel}. We therefore add a \emph{novelty} attribute that measures how new an item is relative to recent same-ticker news, so that duplicates can be down-weighted instead of double-counted.

\textbf{(F2) Missing provenance metadata motivates \emph{credibility}.} Publisher and author fields are absent for 79\% and 68\% of records, but the URL is present for essentially every record. Because reporting factuality varies sharply across outlets \cite{baly2018predicting}, we add a \emph{credibility} attribute inferred primarily from the URL domain and article provenance, with a source whitelist for top-tier financial and official outlets. This recovers a usable source-trust signal despite the missing publisher field.

\textbf{(F3) Broad article scope motivates \emph{scope}.} Sector-, market-, or index-level language appears in 89\% of articles, so many items are not single-stock events. Single-subject extraction is then error-prone: an LLM may attach a market-wide story to one incidental ticker. We add a \emph{scope} attribute (single-stock / sector / market / global) that localizes the impact target, letting downstream models discount mis-attributed subjects rather than trusting every extracted subject equally.

These three attributes are thus \emph{earned} from measured data properties. They primarily improve the fidelity and weighting of the event records; Section~\ref{sec:method} states precisely where each is consumed.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}

\textsc{CausalStock} decomposes news-driven prediction into event extraction, lag-aware event dependency learning, and causal propagation for stock prediction. This decomposition separates three roles often merged in dense text encoders: semantic extraction from news, temporal structure learning across event types, and market prediction under price and ticker context.

\subsection{Phase 1: Structured Event Extraction}

The first module represents each article as a structured event rather than a sentiment score. The 20-class taxonomy (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}) groups event types by market mechanism. A text encoder produces a pooled representation $h_i$; an event-type classifier predicts $\hat{k}_i=\arg\max\,\mathrm{softmax}(W_2\,\mathrm{GELU}(W_1h_i))$; span heads identify subject and object boundaries; and a magnitude head estimates event intensity $\hat{M}_i=W_m\,\mathrm{GELU}(W_c[h_i \Vert E_{\mathrm{type}}(\hat{k}_i)])$.

\textbf{Weak supervision with hybrid corrections.} Large-scale financial news lacks complete event annotations, so we use LLM-produced silver labels \cite{wang2021gpt3,yang2023fingpt} combined with rule- and market-grounded corrections. Before each LLM call we precompute factual anchors and inject them into the prompt to suppress hallucination. \emph{Surprise} is estimated from actual-versus-expected numeric pairs when available,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{surprise}=\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{actual}-\mathrm{expected}}{|\mathrm{expected}|+\epsilon},-1,1\right),
\end{equation}
with a fallback to the immediate price reaction when no explicit expectation is stated. \emph{Novelty} is estimated as one minus the maximum semantic similarity to the previous ten same-ticker items, using FinBERT \texttt{[CLS]} cosine similarity with a Jaccard fallback. After labelling, a post-hoc step overrides LLM estimates with high-confidence numeric values, resets credibility buckets using a source whitelist, and -- when LLM polarity strongly conflicts with the observed price reaction -- lowers credibility rather than flipping the label, preserving the original signal distribution.

\textbf{Where the attributes are used.} Polarity is folded into a signed event magnitude (a negative-polarity event contributes a negative magnitude). Surprise, novelty, scope, and credibility are stored on every event record; novelty and credibility additionally weight events during graph construction and propagation, and scope constrains subject attribution. The evaluated tabular prediction layer (Section~\ref{sec:phase3}) consumes event-type counts and polarity-signed magnitudes; we therefore present novelty, credibility, and scope as extraction-fidelity and weighting improvements rather than as standalone predictive features.

\subsection{Phase 2: Lag-Aware Causal Discovery (STACD)}

The second module learns a global event-type dependency graph from event sequences with a Sparse Temporal Attention Causal Discovery (STACD) network. For event $i$ with text embedding $u_i$, type $k_i$, and timestamp $t_i$, time is encoded with learnable Fourier features $\psi(t_i)$ (64 frequencies), and the event representation is $z_i=W_{\mathrm{in}}[u_i\Vert E_{\mathrm{type}}(k_i)\Vert\psi(t_i)]$. The dependency matrices are constrained transforms of free parameters, $A=\sigma(A_{\mathrm{raw}})$ and $T_{\mathrm{lag}}=\mathrm{softplus}(T_{\mathrm{raw}})$, shared across all attention layers.

For attention from current event $i$ to previous event $j$, the raw score $s_{ij}$ is modulated by a time-direction mask $m_{ij}$ (one only when $t_j<t_i$), a Gaussian lag gate, and the event-type causal strength:
\begin{equation}
g_{ij}=\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\frac{(|t_i-t_j|-T_{\mathrm{lag},k_jk_i})^2}{\sigma_g^2}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\tilde{s}_{ij}=s_{ij}+\log(m_{ij}+\epsilon)+\log(g_{ij}+\epsilon)+\log(A_{k_jk_i}+\epsilon),
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_{ij}=\mathrm{softmax}_j(\tilde{s}_{ij})$. The graph is trained with a prediction loss and structural regularization:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}+\lambda_1\|A\|_1+\lambda_{\mathrm{dag}}\big(\mathrm{tr}(e^{A\odot A})-K\big)^2-\lambda_v\,\mathrm{Var}(A).
\end{equation}
The L1 term discourages dense all-to-all dependencies; the NOTEARS-style term \cite{zheng2018notears} encourages an acyclic event-type graph (the matrix exponential is a 10-term truncated series); the small variance term discourages a degenerate uniform graph. Together they keep the learned structure interpretable for downstream analysis.

\subsection{Phase 3: Prediction Layer}
\label{sec:phase3}

The third module turns the event stream and the learned graph into a movement prediction. On top of the Phase-2 graph we additionally explored a Neural-ODE causal propagation network that integrates per-event impact states in continuous time along the lag-aware graph \cite{chen2018node}; as Section~\ref{sec:results} reports, this stage did not improve prediction, so it is not the featured model and we describe the evaluated layer directly.

For a transparent and reproducible probe, the final classifier is tabular. Each example contains a 32-event context, a 30-day OHLCV window, and a stock identifier, assembled into a feature vector
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_i=[\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{price}}_i\Vert\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{event}}_i\Vert\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{stock}}_i],
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{price}}$ covers recent returns, return volatility, log-volume statistics, and relative OHLC summaries; $\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{event}}$ covers per-type event counts, signed and absolute magnitude sums, the most recent event type, and global magnitude statistics; and $\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{stock}}$ is a one-hot ticker identity. A histogram gradient-boosting classifier predicts class probabilities. We then form a \emph{causal ensemble} that augments these tabular features with features derived from the Phase-2 event-type causal graph (the Phase-3 propagation states are also included but, per Section~\ref{sec:results}, contribute little) and combines several gradient-boosted heads with a meta-model, reported alongside the plain tabular baseline in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Data.} The extraction stage labels roughly 50{,}000 news articles from the 5\% FNSPID subset and, after removing duplicates and non-events, yields 10{,}901 structured event records. The downstream study contains 9{,}566 ticker-aligned examples over 22 stocks spanning 2010--2023. Each example uses 32 historical news events and a 30-day OHLCV window. The class distribution is 39.4\% UP, 43.3\% DOWN, and 17.3\% FLAT.

\textbf{Protocol.} We report macro-F1 and accuracy. Macro-F1 is emphasized because FLAT is the minority class and accuracy can hide imbalance. We use two splits with $70/15/15$ ratios. \emph{Stratified random} splitting measures whether the representation contains reusable information; \emph{chronological} splitting orders examples by time and measures forward-time predictability under distribution shift. Embeddings are precomputed once and cached; the tabular and ensemble heads use a fixed seed, so single-run results should be read as preliminary point estimates.

\textbf{(R1) The causal ensemble improves on a strong tabular baseline.} The tabular model over price, news, and stock-identity features reaches 0.693 macro-F1, far above the three-class random level ($\approx$0.33) and a majority-class predictor (0.201). Augmenting it with Phase-2 event-type causal-graph features and combining several gradient-boosted heads with a meta-model raises macro-F1 to 0.709 and accuracy to 0.734. The 1.6-point macro-F1 gain appears on both validation and test and on two independent feature variants (v1 at 232 dimensions, v2 at 281), so it is consistent; it is nonetheless obtained with a single seed and should be read as a preliminary point estimate pending multi-seed confidence intervals.

\textbf{(R2) The fusion rule is decisive.} On identical features, a single gradient-boosted head scores 0.676 and a hierarchical head 0.683 -- both below the tabular baseline -- while the meta-ensemble scores 0.709. The structured-news increment is real but small enough that the combination rule, not the features alone, determines whether it materializes. This sets a realistic expectation for how much a one-day horizon can extract from news, and it explains why a single weak head can erase the gain.

\textbf{(R3) The Neural-ODE propagation stage did not help and is not featured.} We also evaluated representations from the Phase-3 Neural-ODE causal propagation network on their own. Used directly, the 6{,}596-dimensional ODE hidden states reach only 0.552 macro-F1; an ODE-plus-news variant reaches 0.595 and a distilled fused representation 0.623, all below the 0.693 tabular baseline. Two factors explain this. A representation of several thousand dimensions is not sample-efficient on roughly 6{,}700 training examples and overfits; and daily stock returns have a low signal-to-noise ratio, so a high-capacity continuous-time propagator integrated over a 30-day window tends to fit and amplify noise rather than a stable signal. We therefore do not feature the ODE stage; the gain of the causal ensemble is attributed primarily to the Phase-2 causal-graph features, and a clean ODE-free ablation of the ensemble is left for future work.

\textbf{(R4) Chronological evaluation collapses to near-random.} Table~\ref{tab:split} shows the central limitation. When examples are split by time rather than at random, the tabular model falls from 0.693 to 0.337 macro-F1, near the three-class random baseline; the ODE model collapses similarly to 0.314. The collapse is structural rather than a tuning artifact: on the chronological test set the predicted class counts degenerate toward the majority classes, and the FLAT class is almost never predicted. Because FNSPID spans 2010--2023 -- multiple market regimes -- a model trained on early years does not transfer cleanly to later ones. This is a property of forward-time stock prediction and a market-efficiency effect, not a defect of the news data or the extraction pipeline.

\subsection{Explainability and Intermediate Process}

The pipeline is interpretable at each stage. Phase 1 yields auditable structured records and the attribute-coverage profile in Table~\ref{tab:impact}. Phase 2 yields a sparse event-type graph $A$ and lag matrix $T_{\mathrm{lag}}$ whose top-weighted directed edges can be read as candidate event chains with explicit delays. Phase 3 exposes feature importances from the gradient-boosted classifier: under the stratified split, recent-return and volatility features dominate, followed by stock identity, with event-count and signed-magnitude features contributing a smaller but non-zero share. This ordering is consistent with R1--R2: price history is the strongest single signal, and structured news adds a modest complementary increment rather than replacing it.

\section{Comparison and Discussion}
\label{sec:comparison}

\subsection{Comparison with NeurIPS 2024 CausalStock}

The NeurIPS 2024 CausalStock \cite{li2024causalstock} reports that news consistently helps stock movement prediction. Our causal ensemble also benefits from news, but by a smaller margin, and our chronological results are weaker. Table~\ref{tab:compare} frames the differences along data, method, and protocol axes; we read the gap as an explainable consequence of these axes rather than a contradiction.

\textbf{Data.} We use a 5\% FNSPID subset with 9{,}566 examples and 22 stocks. At this scale the high-capacity propagation and causal modules are sample-starved, as R3 shows. \textbf{Task.} We predict three classes with a $\pm0.5\%$ dead zone; the FLAT class is the hard minority and is exactly what collapses chronologically, whereas a binary up/down task removes this difficulty. \textbf{Method.} Their end-to-end functional causal model couples news with a price-derived causal backbone inside the predictor, while our evaluated layer is a deliberately transparent tabular ensemble over aggregated event statistics, which is interpretable but lossier. \textbf{Protocol.} Our directly comparable number is the chronological one; a stratified random split mixes market regimes across train and test and inflates apparent performance. The two formulations remain complementary: event-type causality is transferable across companies, stock-level causality is specific to a fixed universe.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

Four limitations bound the current results. (i) \emph{Surprise sparsity}: only 6.6\% of events carry a recoverable numeric surprise; price-reaction pseudo-labels and a text-level surprise classifier could raise coverage. (ii) \emph{Single-label extraction}: one article often spans several events, and a top-$k$ multi-label output would preserve compositional structure. (iii) \emph{Temporal generalization}: the chronological collapse is the dominant open problem; regime-aware training and walk-forward validation are the natural next steps. (iv) \emph{Statistical strength}: the 1.6-point ensemble gain is a single-seed point estimate and needs multi-seed confidence intervals, alongside MCC and information-coefficient metrics, before it can be claimed as robust.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper presents \textsc{CausalStock}, an event-structured framework for news-driven stock prediction whose schema is grounded in an exploratory analysis of the data: measured headline duplication, provenance missingness, and broad article scope directly motivate the novelty, credibility, and scope attributes. The pipeline converts roughly 50{,}000 articles into 10{,}901 structured events and learns a lag-aware event-type dependency graph. Empirically, a causal ensemble that fuses price, news, and the learned causal-graph features improves on a strong tabular baseline (0.709 vs.\ 0.693 macro-F1) under stratified evaluation, showing that structured news carries complementary signal. Under chronological evaluation, all models fall to near-random, isolating temporal distribution shift as the central challenge. The contribution is a data-grounded problem formulation, a mathematically specified event-causal model, and an honest analysis that separates information content from genuine forward-time predictability.
